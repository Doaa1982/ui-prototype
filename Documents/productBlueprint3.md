# SECTION III — THE LEARNING HIERARCHY

This section defines the educational model of the platform.

Rather than focusing on isolated content, the platform is built around a structured hierarchy: **Programs** organize **Courses**, Courses organize **Units**, and Units organize **Lessons** — the actual learning sessions students experience.

This model allows the platform to support different teaching styles, subjects, and educational organizations while maintaining a consistent structure for students.

```text
Program

↓

Course

↓

Unit

↓

Lesson
```

---

# 14. Programs

## Vision

Programs provide structure for multiple related Courses.

A program defines a long-term educational journey consisting of one or more courses.

Programs help organizations present complete educational pathways rather than disconnected courses.

Examples include:

- Arabic Language Track
- Data Science Bootcamp
- High School Mathematics
- English for Business
- Front-End Developer Program

---

## Program Structure

```

↓

Course

↓

Unit

↓

```

Programs may lead to:

- Certificates
- Diplomas
- Internal completion badges

# 15. Courses

A Course organizes learning around a specific subject.

Unlike traditional LMS platforms, courses do not own the learning.

Instead, they organize related Units.

Example

```
Program

Arabic Language

↓

Course

Arabic Level A1

↓

Units

↓

Lessons
```

A course provides context, sequencing, and progress rather than acting as a container for files.

---

# 16. Units
Units are the fundamental educational assets of the platform. Programs, Courses, Lessons, Assessments, Analytics, Collaboration, and Educational Intelligence all exist to create, deliver, evaluate, improve, and reuse Units throughout their lifecycle.
Units divide a course into manageable sections.

Each unit focuses on one major topic or skill, and groups the Lessons that teach it.

Example

```
Arabic Level A1

↓

Unit 1

Greetings

↓

Lessons

• Saying Hello
• Introducing Yourself
• Meeting New People
```

Keeping units relatively small allows learners to feel consistent progress.

---

# 17. Lessons

## Vision

A Lesson is the primary educational product created within the platform, and the learning session a student actually experiences.

While a **Unit** acts as the primary *reusable building block* containing curriculum topics, the **Lesson** is the specific *delivery format* and interactive session that students experience inside that Unit.

It represents everything a learner needs to achieve one or more learning objectives within a Unit.

Unlike a traditional video or static content page, a lesson is not limited to one format.

It can combine multiple teaching methods, resources, and activities into a structured learning experience.

The platform should treat Lessons as living educational products that can evolve and improve over time.

---

## Why Lessons?

Most learning platforms are organized around courses alone.

While courses are useful for organizing content, they often fail to describe how students actually learn.

Students do not experience isolated videos or quizzes.

They experience a complete session that includes explanation, practice, feedback, reflection, and assessment.

The platform therefore focuses on designing meaningful Lessons rather than simply publishing educational content.

---

## Lesson Types

Lessons are intentionally flexible. They are not restricted to one content format.

Possible lesson types include:

- Interactive Video
- Live Session
- Reading Lesson
- Presentation
- Whiteboard Lesson
- Audio Lesson
- Coding Exercise
- Discussion
- AI Conversation
- Demonstration
- Workshop

Future lesson types should integrate naturally without changing the overall educational model.

---

## Characteristics

Every Lesson should have:

- Clear learning objectives
- A defined target audience
- An estimated duration
- A structured learning path
- Educational resources
- Interactive activities
- Opportunities for assessment
- Progress tracking
- Continuous improvement

Every lesson should also answer three questions:

- What will students learn?
- How will they learn it?
- How will understanding be measured?

# 18. Activities

Activities transform passive learning into active learning.

Every lesson may contain one or more activities.

Examples include:

- Multiple-choice questions
- Open questions
- Reflection exercises
- Drag and drop
- Flashcards
- Matching exercises
- Fill in the blanks
- Coding challenges
- Speaking practice
- Peer discussion
- Homework
- Projects
- Assessments

Activities should reinforce learning rather than interrupt it.

---

## Activity Flow

```
Explanation

↓

Practice

↓

Feedback

↓

Reflection

↓

```

The objective is to maintain engagement throughout the learning journey.

# 19. Resources

## Vision

Educational knowledge should become reusable.

Instead of recreating the same materials for every lesson, educators should build a growing library of reusable Resources.

Resources are the building blocks of every lesson.

---

## Types of Resources

Examples include:

- Videos
- Activities
- Questions
- Flashcards
- Worksheets
- Homework
- Assessments
- Rubrics
- Images
- Audio
- Documents
- Templates
- AI Prompts
- External Resources

---

## Reusability

A single learning asset may be reused across:

- Multiple lessons
- Multiple units
- Multiple courses
- Multiple programs
- Multiple organizations (if shared)

Improvements made to an asset should be available wherever that asset is used, subject to versioning and publishing rules.

---

## Learning Asset Library

Every Learning Space should gradually build its own educational library.

Over time this becomes one of the organization's most valuable resources.

Instead of creating content repeatedly, educators continuously improve and expand an evolving knowledge base.

The long-term vision is for organizations to accumulate educational expertise that grows in value with every new Lesson they create.
